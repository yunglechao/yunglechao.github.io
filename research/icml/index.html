Which face datasets are worth training on?

Efficient, Validation-Free Intrinsic Quality Estimation for Large-Scale Face Recognition Datasets

Zhichao Chen*, Yongle Zhao*, Kaicheng Yang, Meng Yang, Yin Xie, Ziyong Feng * Equal contribution
Proceedings ↗Paper PDF ↗OpenReview ↗Poster ↗BibTeX ↓

  5. Citation

Adding millions of face images can enrich an embedding space. Incorrect identity labels can also make the space look more diverse. Intrinsic Quality (IQ) combines the spread of embeddings with agreement among nearby labels to rank candidate training sets.

What makes a larger dataset more useful?

We compared WebFace variants under one training and evaluation recipe. Verification accuracy on MFR-ALL after full training gave us a reference ranking. To score a candidate, IQ uses that training set’s proxy embeddings and existing identity labels, without requiring a separate clean validation split.

Read spectral diversity alongside neighborhood labels

We train a proxy face encoder on each candidate dataset and take an identity-stratified sample of about 10,000 images. For each normalized embedding we find its nearest neighbors by cosine similarity, count the fraction sharing its identity label, and average these fractions to obtain Consis.

Normalized effective rank (ER) comes from the eigenvalue distribution of the centered embedding covariance. It rises when more directions share the variance. The main experiments use a fixed score, IQ = 0.2 × Consis + 0.8 × ER. Both trajectories below move to the right, but introducing incorrect labels also sends the trajectory downward.

How do scaling and corruption affect the ranking?

Scaling WebFace from 4M to 42M raised MFR-ALL accuracy from 90.36% to 96.26% and IQ from 0.902 to 0.968. Flipping 40% of the identity labels in WebFace12M also raised effective rank, from 0.916 to 0.994. Yet neighborhood consistency fell from 0.987 to 0.401, alongside a drop in accuracy from 94.37% to 72.01%.

Across the combined scaling and corruption settings, Table 3 reports Spearman rank correlation of 1.000 and Pearson correlation of 0.891 between IQ and MFR-ALL accuracy. IQ preserved the observed order, while a given change in IQ did not translate into a fixed change in accuracy.

Choosing among similarly sized datasets

The authors constructed three roughly 12M-scale candidates using within-identity feature variance. IQ and full-training accuracy ranked them in the same order. This experiment tests selection among variants of one dataset family. Domain shifts and systematic identity merges or splits remain separate questions.
